The challenge

Tax compliance shouldn't require a PhD in spreadsheets

NRIs and Indian residents with foreign equity holdings face a maze of rules — multiple brokers, FIFO matching, STCG vs LTCG, currency conversion, DTAA relief calculations, and Schedule FSI / TR / Form 67 filing requirements. One mistake can mean penalties.

🌐

DTAA explained

A worked example: US dividend, Indian tax

Here is how CapTax AI computes Schedule FSI for a single dividend payout, using sample numbers.

TSLA — Tesla Inc • Sample dividend payout

Gross dividend (USD 1,000 × ₹83.50) + ₹83,500
Foreign tax withheld (25% US withholding) ₹20,875
Indian tax (30% of gross) ₹25,050
DTAA relief (= foreign tax withheld, capped at Indian tax) − ₹20,875
Net Indian tax payable ₹4,175
Net Indian tax = (Gross × 30%) − min(Gross × 25%, Gross × 30%)   ≈ 5% of gross dividend

Security & privacy

What we actually do to protect your data

httpOnly session cookies

Sessions are stored as signed, httpOnly, SameSite=lax cookies — not accessible to JavaScript, protecting against XSS.

Hashed credentials

Passwords are stored only as bcrypt salted hashes. Plaintext never appears anywhere in the codebase or logs.

When you sell shares, the pipeline matches them against your oldest buy lots first (FIFO). Corporate actions like stock splits and bonuses are adjusted in the adjusted_trades.csv step before matching, so the cost basis reflects the real economic position.
The pipeline uses the India–USA DTAA treaty rate of 25% withholding on dividends. The DTAA relief equals the foreign tax withheld (capped at the Indian tax liability), resulting in a net Indian tax of approximately 5% of gross for a 30% Indian rate.
All pipeline steps run on your own machine at http://localhost:8001. No trade data is transmitted to external servers. The 3D coin hero on this page loads Three.js from unpkg.com CDN, which is the only external network request.
